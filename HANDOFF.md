# LeanSolid handoff

The initial milestone covers small-strain isotropic elasticity. A lecture-driven extension adds tensor algebra and derivatives, constitutive energy and compliance, weak equilibrium and Galerkin estimates, planar reductions and selected analytic results.

| Source | Implemented |
|---|---|
| `LeanSolid/Math/` | Cartesian vectors and second-order tensors reuse Mathlib functions and matrices over `Fin n`. The symmetric part and Frobenius contraction are defined, with basic symmetry, linearity and positivity properties. |
| `LeanSolid/LinearElasticity/Definitions.lean` | Pointwise small strain from a supplied displacement gradient, Lamé's stress law and quadratic strain-energy density. |
| `LeanSolid/LinearElasticity/Theorems.lean` | Strain symmetry, stress linearity, the stress trace formula and energy nonnegativity. |
| `LeanSolid/LinearElasticity/Corollaries.lean` | Stress for trace-free input and pointwise recovery of strain from stress. |
| `LeanSolid/Elastostatics/` | Weak equilibrium, reciprocal work, minimum potential energy, Galerkin estimates, and existence and uniqueness for the bounded clamped Sobolev model. |
| `LeanSolid/Constitutive/Uniaxial.lean` and `LeanSolid/Examples/Uniaxial.lean` | Existing scalar example and original import paths. |

## Assumptions and open work

- The [upstream attribution record](docs/LINEAR_ELASTICITY_UPSTREAM.md) identifies the pinned lean-mechanics revision, source files, and the LeanSolid adaptations used for Linear Elasticity 1.

- The local, Git-ignored course directory holds the Brown ENGN 2240 materials and internal lecture review.

- Tensor components use a fixed orthonormal Cartesian basis. The [Cartesian bridge](LeanSolid/LinearElasticity/Cartesian.lean) identifies the matrix strain with the entries of the symmetric Euclidean derivative and specializes the rigid-motion theorem. The [Cartesian compatibility theorem](LeanSolid/LinearElasticity/CartesianCompatibility.lean) proves equivalence between second-partial and operator constraints and recovers a Cartesian displacement on an open star-shaped domain. The strain field is assumed globally C² and symmetric, and compatibility is imposed only on the domain.
- The pointwise stress law accepts all real Lamé parameters. On symmetric three-dimensional strains, strict energy positivity holds exactly for `0 < mu` and `0 < 3 * lam + 2 * mu`. Pointwise strain recovery uses `mu ≠ 0` and `n * lam + 2 * mu ≠ 0`.
- Weak equilibrium uses a complete real inner-product space and a continuous load. The elastic-form theorem derives coercivity from a uniformly positive stiffness operator and a Korn estimate. The clamped model defines H¹₀ as the closure of smooth compactly supported displacement–gradient pairs, proves Korn and Poincaré estimates, and handles continuous loads including an L² body force for μ > 0 and λ + μ ≥ 0. This includes the full three-dimensional positive energy range. The [inhomogeneous Dirichlet theorem](LeanSolid/Elastostatics/Sobolev/Dirichlet/Theorems.lean) reuses homogeneous existence through a load shift. It assumes a supplied displacement–gradient lift and proves invariance under changing the lift by an element of H¹₀. The trace and extension characterization and pure traction remain open. For mixed boundaries, [conditional weak existence](LeanSolid/Elastostatics/Sobolev/Mixed.lean) uses a supplied continuous face trace and a Korn estimate on the variation space. [Fixed face rigidity](LeanSolid/Elastostatics/FixedFace.lean) eliminates affine rigid modes. [Endpoint trace](LeanSolid/Math/Calculus/EndpointTrace.lean) bounds evaluation on smooth normal slices. [Face trace](LeanSolid/Math/Calculus/FaceTrace.lean) now integrates the estimate over a smooth scalar planar rectangle using Fubini and proves uniqueness of continuous extension from a dense family with a bulk norm bound. [Vector L² face trace](LeanSolid/Math/Calculus/FaceTraceL2.lean) assembles component estimates and proves unique continuous top trace extension for a supplied dense smooth graph and linear evaluation satisfying the bulk norm bound. The trace module now proves the density input for any closed graph range and the L² membership of jointly C¹ rectangle components, including top evaluation and the normal derivative. [Rectangle trace](LeanSolid/Elastostatics/Sobolev/RectangleTrace.lean) constructs the two-component smooth bottom-fixed graph with both derivative columns, completes it by closure, and gives the unique continuous top trace and a continuous L² traction load. [Rectangle weak form](LeanSolid/Elastostatics/Sobolev/RectangleWeak.lean) assembles the strain, divergence and Lamé form on that graph and proves conditional existence and uniqueness. The bottom-fixed Poincaré estimate bounds the displacement by its normal derivative, reducing the remaining uniform Korn estimate on smooth trials to control of the full gradient by the symmetric gradient. The established closure theorem transfers such an estimate to the completed graph. [Compact remainder reduction](LeanSolid/Elastostatics/Sobolev/RectangleKorn.lean) is an alternative route: second Korn, compactness of the bulk displacement projection, and a trivial strain kernel on the completed space imply first Korn. The concrete strain kernel premise is now proved directly: componentwise bottom Poincaré sets the normal displacement to zero, the tangential weak identity and smooth-test separation remove its tangential derivative, and the shear equation and bottom Poincaré then set the remaining component and gradient to zero. The concrete displacement projection is now compact. The second Korn estimate with the lower-order L² displacement term is now proved for the completed bottom-fixed rectangle graph. [One-face elastic reflection](LeanSolid/Elastostatics/Korn/Reflection.lean) now gives a C¹ gluing with matching first derivatives, proves cancellation of skew-gradient terms in the reflected strain, and bounds its squared integral on the reflected strip. The cutoff product estimate accounts for the lower-order displacement term. [Four-edge extension](LeanSolid/Elastostatics/Korn/RectangleExtension.lean) now constructs and verifies the C¹ extension across the full rectangle, invariance of the strain energy under the needed axis changes, and a geometry-dependent compact cutoff preserving the interior field and derivative. The integrated strain of the four-edge extension on the enlarged rectangle is bounded by 18433² times the original strain integral when three times the extension depth fits in both dimensions. The squared displacement integral is bounded by 4609² times the original under the same width conditions. The compact cutoff extension now has a whole-plane strain integral bound in terms of the original strain and displacement integrals. The [mollifier lemmas](LeanSolid/Math/Calculus/Mollifier.lean) prove differentiation under convolution, pointwise derivative convergence, a common compact support for bounded radii, and a uniform derivative bound. Dominated convergence gives the whole-space Korn identity for compactly supported C¹ fields. A measure-preserving coordinate change and the cutoff extension give the planar integral estimate, which transfers to the completed graph. The second Korn closure lemma transfers a bound on smooth trials to the completed rectangle graph. [Rectangle compactness](LeanSolid/Elastostatics/Sobolev/RectangleCompactness.lean) proves the one-dimensional cell-average error bound using the existing mean-zero Wirtinger theorem adapted from lean-mechanics, and connects finite-dimensional approximations converging in operator norm to the required compactness via Mathlib. [Rectangle averaging](LeanSolid/Elastostatics/Sobolev/RectangleAveraging.lean) now gives disjoint half-open uniform cells and concrete continuous finite-rank averaging/reconstruction, with exact averages and almost-everywhere reconstruction on each cell. The cells cover the rectangle almost everywhere, and bulk integrals split as finite sums over cells. A uniform cellwise error bound now assembles into the corresponding bulk bound. For the local two-dimensional estimate, squared interval averages are now bounded by averages of squares. The pointwise cell residual splits into a horizontal slice residual and the horizontal average of the vertical residual. The integrated cell theorem applies Fubini and both interval derivative estimates. The assembled grid error carries over to the completed trial graph and gives operator-norm convergence at order 1/k. The resulting compactness and the verified second Korn estimate give existence and uniqueness for the bottom-fixed, top-traction rectangle with μ > 0, λ ≥ 0, zero lateral traction in the weak formulation, and any continuous bulk load. The result is available as `existsUnique_rectangle_weak_solution`. The rectangle graph now satisfies normal and tangential weak integration by parts against smooth tests vanishing on the relevant integration edges, including interior tests. The bottom trace characterization remains open independently of the direct strain-kernel proof. The previously established rigidity result concerns classical rigid motions.
- [Airy representation](LeanSolid/Plane/Airy/Representation.lean) now recovers an Airy potential from differentiable equilibrated stresses on an open star-shaped planar domain. For C² stresses with compatible isotropic compliance strains, the potential is C⁴ on the domain and biharmonic. The converse uses local smoothness on any open planar domain. The proof reuses the LeanSolid star-shaped Poincaré lemma and existing Airy identities, with Mathlib bump functions for localization. Extension to arbitrary simply connected domains, global periods on domains with holes, Goursat representation and elastic Green tensors remain open.

- [Circular Eshelby inclusion](LeanSolid/Plane/Eshelby/Corollaries.lean) verifies the radial plane-strain solution for equal Lamé moduli and a uniform in-plane dilatational eigenstrain with zero axial eigenstrain. It derives the amplitudes from displacement and traction matching, proves radial equilibrium and far-field decay, and gives uniform interior strain. The uniqueness claim is for the two radial amplitudes. Literature context is [Eshelby (1957)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1957.0133). The proof reuses the existing LeanSolid radial elasticity lemmas.

- The future checks on multiplicative elastoplastic kinematics and the HRR exponent sit outside this linear-elastic milestone.

## Local build

With Lean 4.36.0, run `lake exe cache get` and `lake build` from the repository. This Windows checkout reuses the same Mathlib revision from the surrounding Lean workspace via a local junction at the ignored `.lake/packages` path. A fresh checkout uses normal Lake dependency setup.
